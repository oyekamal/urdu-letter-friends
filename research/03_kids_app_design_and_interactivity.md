# 03 — Kids' App Design, Benchmarks, Lottie Interactivity, Safety, and Project Rules

*Research note for urdu-letter-friends (ages 3–6, animated Urdu letter characters). Compiled 2026-09-21. Claims marked **[unverified]** could not be confirmed against a primary source and should be checked before being relied on.*

---

## 1. Engagement design for 3–6: what makes preschoolers come back (without manipulating them)

**What the evidence says works.** Sesame Workshop's *Best Practices: Designing Touch Tablet Experiences for Preschoolers* (50+ touchscreen studies) is still the most concrete guide. Its core findings: familiar characters used as "hosts" create "extended engagement and dialogue between the child and the character"; "payoffs are very important to children" and should reflect the curricular concept and the child's choice ("Nice job choosing the letter A!") with sound effect plus animation; children "expect immediate feedback from their touch" and tap harder/longer/repeatedly until they see it, so register input **on touch-down, not lift**; assume the child cannot read and convey every instruction verbally *and* visually; hotspots "must be large and adequately isolated" and a glow/sparkle "time-out" after 3–5 s (stories) or 6–8 s (games) of inactivity should hint what to tap; wrong answers get three escalating scaffolds (encourage → hint → highlight the answer); horizontal scrolling beats vertical; keep active icons away from the bottom edge where wrists rest; landscape is the natural hold. ([Sesame Workshop 2012, PDF](https://joanganzcooneycenter.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/02/SesameWorkshop-2012.pdf))

The **Designing for Children's Rights Guide 2.0** (2022) adds ten principles grouped into Inclusion, Play & Learning, Safety & Sustainability: gather children's views, everyone can use, communication children understand, **allow and support exploration**, encourage play with others, **create a balanced environment**, keep children safe, don't misuse data, help children recognise commercial activity, design for the future. ([D4CR via Cooney Center](https://joanganzcooneycenter.org/2022/09/26/the-designing-for-childrens-rights-guide/)) **UNICEF's RITEC** framework (2024) names eight well-being components digital play should serve: safety and security; diversity, equity and inclusion; social connection; emotional regulation; self-actualisation; empowerment (autonomy/agency); creativity; competence. ([UNICEF Innocenti RITEC report, PDF](https://www.unicef.dk/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/ritec_responsible-innovation-in-technology-for-children-digital-technology-play-and-child-well-being.pdf)) Read together, the "return engine" for this age is: a character with personality who reacts to *me*, cause-and-effect toys, real choice over what to poke next, a growing collection I can revisit, and a parent nearby to co-play.

**What the evidence says to avoid.** A JAMA Network Open study of 133 apps used by 160 children aged 3–5 found roughly **80% contained manipulative design**; children from lower-SES homes met significantly more of it. Four categories: *parasocial pressure* (a character says "Do you want to give up?"), *fabricated time pressure* (countdowns, "limited time"), *navigation constraints* (auto-advance, buried exit, hard opt-out — 45.9% of apps), and *lures* (sparkles/badges to prolong play — 45.1%). ([Meyer et al. 2022, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9206186/)) 5Rights' *Disrupted Childhood* covers the same persuasive-design catalogue (streaks, autoplay, variable rewards). ([5Rights, PDF](https://5rightsfoundation.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/5rights_DisruptedChildhood_G.pdf)) Common Sense Media filed FTC comments asking for guidance against dark patterns aimed at children. ([Common Sense 2022 FTC comment](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/sites/default/files/featured-content/files/common-sense-dark-patterns-comment.pdf))

**Session length.** The AAP recommends up to one hour/day of high-quality media for ages 2–5, co-viewed where possible. ([CNLD summary of AAP](https://www.cnld.org/how-long-should-a-childs-attention-span-be/)) Educator rules of thumb put focused attention at roughly 2–5 minutes per year of age, i.e. **6–15 min for a 3-year-old, 10–25 for a 5-year-old**, highly variable and activity-dependent. ([firstschool.net](https://www.firstschool.net/blog/understanding-attention-spans-in-preschoolers)) Design a natural stopping point every **3–8 minutes** (one letter's "story" is done; the character yawns and waves) rather than an engineered reason to stay.

**Tap targets.** Anthony et al. tested 3.2/6.4/9.5/12.7 mm targets on a 4-inch Android phone: children missed 23.1% of first taps vs 16.9% for adults, and produced **96% of all "holdover" taps** (touching where the previous target was). Android's baseline is 7–10 mm; children need "substantially larger". ([Anthony et al. 2012, PDF](https://lisa-anthony.com/wp-content/uploads/2012/09/anthony-et-al-tabletop20121.pdf)) The project's **≥ 2 cm (~75 px at 160 dpi / 48+ dp with generous padding)** target and **≥ 8 mm gap** between targets is a deliberate over-provision above those numbers **[project heuristic, not a published standard]**.

### Healthy hooks vs dark patterns

| Healthy hook (build) | Dark pattern (never) | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Character reacts differently to the 2nd/3rd tap (surprise, not random slot-machine) | Variable-ratio loot / mystery boxes | Curiosity vs compulsion; lures were in 45% of preschool apps (JAMA) |
| Sticker/letter-friend collection that is always complete-able, no expiry | Streaks, daily-login bonuses, "come back tomorrow" | Fabricated time pressure targets a child who can't tell time |
| Character says goodbye and the app sits quietly | Character pleads "don't go!" / "are you sure?" on exit | Parasocial pressure |
| Free choice of which letter to poke next | Locked linear path with gated content | D4CR "allow exploration"; RITEC autonomy |
| Wrong tap → gentle hint, 3 scaffolds, never "fail" | Lives, hearts, timers, game-over | Sesame "learning moment"; no fear loop |
| Audio payoff tied to the letter sound | Confetti + fanfare for every tap regardless | Reward should teach, not just excite |
| Parent tips screen behind a reading gate | Cross-sell, external links, ads in the child path | Google Families policy; Sesame "baby gate" |
| One natural end per 3–8 min session | Autoplay to next activity | Navigation constraints (45.9% of apps) |

## 2. Benchmark: preschool literacy apps

| App | Core loop | How a letter is taught | On tap | Reward | Parent gate | Monetisation | Rating / complaints |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Endless Alphabet** (Originator) | Pick word from monster's mouth → drag letters home → watch definition animation | Letters are talking monsters; each drag says the *phoneme*; word animation shows meaning | Letter wiggles + says its sound | Intrinsic: the animation itself; new words added | Cross-links behind a "security screen nonreaders can't pass" | Free trial words → one-time IAP (7 free, ~100 unlock) | CSM 3+; "more a vocabulary app than an alphabet app"; needs internet + big storage |
| **Khan Academy Kids** | Guided path or free library; Kodi the bear + animal guides | Letter recognition, blending, leveled books, videos | Task-specific feedback | Choose a prize for your animal friend's collection after each activity | Multi-profile parent area, light reporting | **Free, no ads, no IAP** (non-profit) | CSM 5★ top-rated; "technical glitches" |
| **Duolingo ABC** | 127 linear units; finish one to unlock next | One letter at a time: trace, hear sound, upper/lower, first words | Tap/drag/speak; clear instructions | Progression itself (no streaks noted in review) | Needs child's name, mic permission | Free | CSM 3+; can't skip levels; "unnecessary repetition"; best "in small doses" |
| **Lingokids** | 4000+ games/songs/shows across A1–B1 | English vocab games, videos, podcasts | Game-specific | Levels + collectibles; screen-time limits in parent area | Full parent dashboard | Free tier ≈ 6 min then wall; $13.49/mo or $71.88/yr | Ads: none; complaints on billing/cancellation, repetition |
| **Sago Mini World** | Digital toy box; no goals | Not literacy — pure cause-and-effect play with Harvey, Jinja | Everything reacts; open-ended | None: "no high scores, no timers, no Game Over" | No hidden menus or tiny X → browser | Subscription, no ads | Ages 2–6; kids "age out" |
| **Sesame Street Alphabet Kitchen** | Elmo (vowels) / Cookie Monster (blending) bake letter cookies | Synthetic phonics: combine sounds into 3–4-letter words | Cookie-cutter letter → dough letter; Elmo names the letter | Decorate the cookie, choose who to give it to | Standard Sesame parent section | Paid app | Phonics.org: Elmo "does not articulate the letter sound itself"; no profiles, no letter choice |
| **Urdu/Arabic on Play Store**: Alif Bay Pay, Kids Urdu Qaida, Alif Bay Jeem, Basic Urdu Qaida, Taleemabad, Sabaq MUSE, Noor Kids | Flashcard grid → tap letter → hear name + 1–3 objects; some tracing | Letter name (rarely the phoneme), object pictures, static art | Sound plays; "big letters and animations associated with each letter" (Kids Urdu Qaida) | Mostly none; balloon-pop/fishing mini-games (Basic Urdu Qaida) | Rare | Ad-supported free apps **[ads/IAP per app unverified — Play pages blocked during research]** | Taleemabad Student App 4.09★ / 7.4k ratings, Android 4.4+ (AppBrain snippet); Urdu qaida apps are flashcard-style, not character-driven |

**Endless Alphabet** is the closest cousin to this project: letters *are* characters, the phoneme is spoken on every drag, and the payoff is a short animated skit. Its weaknesses — online-only, heavy downloads, vocabulary skewed to English — are exactly the gaps to exploit. ([CSM review](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/app-reviews/endless-alphabet), [Screenwise](https://screenwiseapp.com/media/endless-alphabet-game))

**Khan Academy Kids** shows a healthy collection mechanic: the prize goes to the *animal friend*, not to a score, and the app is free with zero ads. It also shows the cost of breadth: a giant library that needs guidance. ([CSM review](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/app-reviews/khan-academy-kids))

**Duolingo ABC** proves that explicit, systematic phonics with tracing works in a free app, but its rigid linear path is the most-cited complaint — preschoolers who already know a letter are forced through it. ([CSM review](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/app-reviews/duolingo-abc-learn-to-read))

**Lingokids** is the cautionary tale on monetisation: high-quality content, no ads, but a ~6-minute free wall and recurring billing complaints. ([techpoint.africa](https://techpoint.africa/guide/lingokids-tested-review/), [kudokids](https://kudokids.org/compare/lingokids))

**Sago Mini World** is the model for *feel*: no timers, no scores, consistent characters, everything tappable reacts. Adopt its interaction philosophy, add the letter curriculum on top. ([Screenwise](https://screenwiseapp.com/media/sago-mini-world-game))

**Sesame Street Alphabet Kitchen** warns about a subtle pedagogy bug this project must not repeat: tapping a letter gives its *name* but not its *sound*. ([Phonics.org](https://cms.phonics.org/sesame-street-alphabet-kitchen-app-review/))

**Urdu apps on Play Store** (Alif Bay Pay, Kids Urdu Qaida, Alif Bay Jeem, Basic Urdu Qaida) are largely flashcard grids with a name recording and 1–3 object pictures; one adds tracing, one adds balloon-pop mini-games. None found makes the letter itself a character, none is documented as offline-first, and store listings suggest ad-supported free models. Taleemabad and Sabaq MUSE are broader curriculum apps (animated video lessons, KG–5) rather than alphabet toys. ([Alif Bay Jeem](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.LearnWithFun.AlifBayJeem), [Kids Urdu Qaida](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.urduqaida), [Basic Urdu Qaida](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.littletreehouse.urduqaida), [Taleemabad on AppBrain](https://www.appbrain.com/app/taleemabad-student-app/com.orenda.taimo.myapplication), [MUSE](https://apkpure.com/muse-learning-app-for-kg-5/com.sabaq.Muse5)) The market gap is real: an ad-free, offline, character-first Urdu alphabet app does not appear to exist.

## 3. Interactive Lottie: the tech options

**lottie-web (airbnb).** `loadAnimation({renderer: 'svg'|'canvas'|'html', loop, autoplay, path|animationData})`; control with `playSegments([from,to], forceFlag)`, `goToAndStop`, `setSpeed`, `setDirection`; listen to `complete`, `loopComplete`, `enterFrame`, `segmentStart`. Named markers exported from After Effects (or authored in JSON `markers[]`) let you address segments by name instead of frame numbers. Canvas is usually cheaper than SVG on mobile ("if you can use canvas, it's usually more efficient"). ([lottie-web README](https://github.com/airbnb/lottie-web), [issue #1327](https://github.com/airbnb/lottie-web/issues/1327), [named markers](https://lottiefiles.com/blog/working-with-lottie-animations/how-to-setup-named-markers-in-lottie-animations)) `lottie-interactivity` adds scroll/cursor sync but is aimed at web scroll effects, not tap-toys. ([GitHub](https://github.com/LottieFiles/lottie-interactivity))

**dotLottie state machines (LottieFiles).** The `.lottie` container (zip of JSON + assets + optional state machines) supports a finite state machine spec: states, guarded transitions, tweened transitions, boolean/numeric/string inputs, and events; pointer events (`PointerDown/Up/Enter/Exit`, `Click`, `OnComplete`) are auto-wired to the canvas with hit-testing on layers. API: `stateMachineLoad(id)`, `stateMachineStart()`, `stateMachineFireEvent(name)`, `stateMachineSetBooleanInput(...)`. ([dotLottie spec 2.0](https://dotlottie.io/spec/2.0/), [dotlottie-web wiki](https://github.com/LottieFiles/dotlottie-web/wiki/State-Machines)) The same file runs in `dotlottie-web`/`dotlottie-react`, `dotlottie-flutter` (state machines, events, theming; pointer events forwarded from Flutter, listeners attached only for declared events), `dotlottie-react-native` (New Architecture, state machines on iOS/Android/web), and native Android/iOS. ([dotlottie-flutter](https://github.com/lottiefiles/dotlottie-flutter), [LottieFiles blog](https://lottiefiles.com/blog/working-with-lottie-animations/the-flutter-dotlottie-glow-up), [dotlottie-react-native docs](https://docs.lottiefiles.com/en/runtimes/distributions/react-native)) This is the natural fit for "tap the letter → it reacts": one `.lottie` per letter with `idle`, `tap1`, `tap2`, `surprise`, `sleep` states.

**Rive as the alternative.** Rive's editor authors vector rigs + state machines natively; runtimes expose triggers/booleans/numbers, hit areas, and (in Flutter 0.14 stable) `RiveWidgetController` with `hitTestBehavior`. Callstack's React Native benchmark on an old Xperia Z3: **Rive ~60 fps vs Lottie ~17 fps**, Rive file ~2 KB vs Lottie ~24 KB, though Rive used more GPU memory (184 vs 123 MB). ([Callstack](https://www.callstack.com/blog/lottie-vs-rive-optimizing-mobile-app-animation), [Rive Flutter docs](https://rive.app/docs/runtimes/flutter/flutter), [0.14 stable](https://community.rive.app/c/announcements/flutter-runtime-0-14-0-is-now-stable)) Prefer Rive when you need bone rigs, blend-states (a face that tracks a finger), or dozens of simultaneously animated characters on 2 GB phones. Prefer Lottie/dotLottie when assets are generated from code (this repo's `lotties/` pipeline, python-lottie), when you want an open JSON format with no editor lock-in, or when the interaction is "tap → play a segment". Rive's editor has a free tier (≈3 files per one listing) then paid plans from ~$9–14/mo **[pricing from third-party listings, unverified]**. LottieFiles' own 2026 comparison concedes Rive is "the go-to for complex, stateful interactivity" while dotLottie state machines have closed much of the gap. ([LottieFiles blog](https://lottiefiles.com/blog/lottie-animations/lottiefiles-or-rive))

**Low-end Android reality (Pakistan).** Sub-PKR-20k phones (Tecno Pop, Infinix Smart, itel) ship with **2–4 GB RAM, 32–64 GB storage, Android Go**; Infinix + itel alone were reported near half of 2025 shipments. ([gilgit.app](https://gilgit.app/blog/top-budget-phones-in-pakistan-under-20-000-pkr-for-2025/), [itel-pk](https://itel-pk.com/blogs/seo-blogs/android-mobile-price-in-pakistan-2025-best-phones-under-10-000-to-15-000-itel-picks), [LinkedIn market note, unverified](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/jawad-akhtar-81a07016_here-are-the-top-10-smartphone-market-trends-activity-7431095423823503360-FQXv)) Statcounter (Aug 2026, PK mobile): Android 13 18.4%, 11 15.2%, 15 12.6%, 16 12.1%, 12 11.3%, 14 9.7% — a long tail of Android 11–12 devices. ([Statcounter](https://gs.statcounter.com/android-version-market-share/mobile-tablet/pakistan)) Consequences: target **minSdk 24–26**, one animated character on screen at a time, ≤ 2 Lottie instances alive, canvas renderer or native (Skia/Impeller via Flutter) rather than SVG DOM, no masks/mattes/gaussian blur in letter rigs, precomposed shapes with matched vertex counts for morphs (see `lottie-motion` skill).

**Budgets.** Per-letter `.lottie` ≤ 60 KB compressed (Lottie JSON ~3–4× a Rive file, so keep paths simple); per-letter audio ≤ 40 KB (Opus/OGG 24 kbps mono); whole app incl. 38 letters + mnemonic words + voice ≤ 25 MB install so it fits on a 32 GB phone with 4 GB free. **[budgets are project targets]**

**Recommended stack: Flutter + dotlottie-flutter (Rive as upgrade path), offline-first.**
- *Why not PWA:* iOS/Android WebViews on 2 GB phones give ~17 fps on lottie-web-class rendering (Callstack's numbers were RN-bridged but the JS-side cost is the same), audio autoplay is gated, Play Store "Designed for Families" listing and Teacher Approved badge require a real APK, and a PWA can't ship a Nastaliq font reliably across OEM browsers. Keep the web build only as a demo/preview.
- *Why not React Native:* extra JS bridge on already-weak CPUs; dotlottie-react-native is newer and still logging state-machine bugs in community threads. ([lottie.community bug thread](https://www.lottie.community/c/bugs/error-with-lottiefiles-dotlottie-react-native))
- *Why Flutter:* single codebase, Skia/Impeller native rendering, first-party `dotlottie-flutter` with state machines and forwarded pointer events, `rive` 0.14 stable if a rig outgrows Lottie, Play Asset Delivery / on-device bundles for offline, and `flutter_tts`-free design (all audio pre-recorded). Flutter APK floor is ~5–7 MB, acceptable inside the 25 MB cap. **[verify APK size against actual build]**

## 4. Accessibility and safety

**COPPA (US, <13).** 2025 amendments (effective 23 Jun 2025, full compliance by 22 Apr 2026): separate verifiable parental **opt-in** before any disclosure to third parties for advertising; data retention limited to what is "reasonably necessary"; written security programme; biometrics added to personal information. The clean path for this app is **collect nothing**: no accounts, no analytics SDK by default, local-only progress. ([FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/01/ftc-finalizes-changes-childrens-privacy-rule-limiting-companies-ability-monetize-kids-data), [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/04/22/2025-05904/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule))

**GDPR-K (EU) / UK Children's Code.** Article 8 sets the digital consent age at 16 by default, member states may lower to 13; the ICO code expects age-band-appropriate design (0–5, 6–9…) and high-privacy defaults. Again, zero collection sidesteps consent machinery. ([bastion.tech summary](https://bastion.tech/learn/gdpr/childrens-data-protection/), [Fenwick on ICO code](https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/the-ico-age-appropriate-design-code-of-practice-steps-to-take-in-2021-to-protect-childrens-privacy))

**Google Play Families policy.** Declare target age in Play Console; child-directed apps may use only **Families Self-Certified Ads SDKs** (programme currently closed to new applicants) with no personalised ads, no interstitials at launch, ads dismissible in 5 s; must not transmit AAID/IMEI/MAC/SSID etc.; no location permission; no links violating policy; only approved APIs/SDKs; virtual-currency vs real-money distinction must be clear; compliant apps can opt into **Teacher Approved**. Simplest compliance: **no ads, no IAP, no external links in the child path**; put parent info behind a reading gate (e.g. "tap the three digits: ۳ ۷ ۲" spelled in Urdu words). ([Families policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9893335), [Ads SDK programme](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/12955712))

**Colour-blind-safe palette.** Use the Okabe–Ito set (vermilion #FF2000/orange #F57F17, yellow #FFD700, green #009E73, sky #56B4E9, blue #0072B2, purple #CC79A7), never encode meaning in red-vs-green alone, always pair colour with shape/sound. ([Okabe & Ito](https://jfly.uni-koeln.de/color/)) Highlight colour for "tappable" should be a single consistent bright yellow/neon-green glow, per Sesame.

**Left-handed tracing.** Urdu is written right-to-left, so a right-hand palm naturally covers the stroke start. Mirror the demo hand for left/right (parent setting or auto-detect from first strokes), place undo/skip on the *opposite* side of the dominant hand, and accept partial strokes (Sesame: "support partial completion").

**Urdu font rendering on Android.** Nastaliq is the culturally correct script, but Noto Nastaliq Urdu on Android "was consuming far more vertical space than the actual text appeared to need", causing gaps and clipped marks; developers fix with `includeFontPadding=false`, explicit `lineHeight`, and metric overrides, and web toggles report line-height clipping too. ([proandroiddev, Aug 2026 — body 403'd, snippet only](https://proandroiddev.com/taming-noto-nastaliq-fixing-urdu-font-rendering-issues-in-android-c65e83b40b34), [r/Urdu](https://www.reddit.com/r/Urdu/comments/1qeqxfj/urdu_fonts_looked_weird_in_chrome_so_i_made_a/)) Two historic reader surveys (1927 al-Hilal, 1959 Nawa-i-Waqt) favoured Naskh for legibility, and Naskh is what beginners find easier — but Pakistani schoolbooks and children's expectations are Nastaliq. ([pakistanlink commentary](https://pakistanlink.org/Commentary/2025/Dec25/12/06.HTM), [r/Urdu](https://www.reddit.com/r/Urdu/comments/1md56yx/is_it_bad_and_weird_i_find_difficulty_in_nastaliq/)) Decision for this project: **letters are vector shapes inside the Lottie, not live text**, so rendering is identical on every device; the few live-text labels (parent screen, word captions) use Noto Nastaliq Urdu with `line-height ≥ 2.2` and clipping tests on a 720p Android 11 emulator, with Noto Naskh Arabic as the fallback for tone-mark-heavy words. Isolated letter forms (initial/medial/final) are taught as separate animation states, not via font shaping.

## 5. Design rules for this project (checkable)

1. Every tap produces a sound within **100 ms** and a visible reaction within **1 frame (≤ 33 ms)**; input registers on **touch-down**, not release.
2. **Letter sound (phoneme) plays before the letter name, and before the mnemonic word**, on every first tap of a session. (Fix the Alphabet Kitchen bug.)
3. Tappable elements are **≥ 2 cm (≥ 75 px @ 160 dpi)** with **≥ 8 mm** clearance; nothing tappable in the bottom 12% of the screen.
4. **No text menus in the child path.** Every navigation element is an icon + spoken label; the parent screen is the only place with sentences.
5. **No timers, streaks, lives, hearts, daily bonuses, countdowns, or "come back tomorrow"** anywhere.
6. **No character ever asks the child to keep playing or expresses sadness at exit.** Exit = character waves and yawns, app goes quiet.
7. Reactions on repeated taps follow a **fixed, authored sequence** (tap1 → tap2 → tap3 → rest), never a random reward schedule; the sequence resets after 10 s idle.
8. Every letter is reachable from the home board at all times; **no locked or greyed letters**.
9. Collection (stickers/friends) is **always completable**, has no expiry, and rewards *doing* (tap, trace, hear) not *time spent*.
10. After **6–8 s** of inactivity, one hint glow on one tappable; after 20 s the character models the action once; never auto-advance.
11. Wrong tracing follows Sesame's 3-step scaffold (encourage → hint → guided path) and **always ends in success**.
12. A natural stopping point occurs every **3–8 minutes**: letter story complete → "phir milenge" → static home board with no prompt.
13. **Zero network calls in the child path**; app fully functional offline with all 38 letters, sounds and words bundled ≤ 25 MB.
14. **No ads, no IAP, no external links** in the child path; the single parent link is behind a Urdu-numeral reading gate.
15. **No analytics, accounts, device identifiers or permissions** except vibration (optional) and storage for local progress; privacy policy says exactly that.
16. Colour never carries meaning alone; palette is Okabe–Ito-derived; the "tappable" highlight is one consistent colour app-wide.
17. Tracing supports **partial strokes**, both hands (mirrored demo), and is right-to-left with correct Urdu stroke order.
18. Letters in animation are **vector shapes**, not font text; live Urdu text uses Noto Nastaliq with line-height ≥ 2.2 and passes a clipping check on Android 11 / 720p.
19. **≤ 2 Lottie/Rive instances alive** at once; each letter `.lottie` ≤ 60 KB; no masks, mattes, blurs or image layers in character rigs.
20. Every letter rig exposes the same **state set** (`idle`, `tap1`, `tap2`, `tap3`, `sleep`, `celebrate`) so the engine treats all 38 identically.
21. Sustained **≥ 30 fps on a 2 GB / Android 11 Go device** (Tecno Pop 7 or equivalent) in the letter screen, verified before every release.
22. Landscape-first layout; all key interactives visible **without scrolling**; if a strip must scroll it scrolls horizontally.
23. A **parent co-play screen** (behind the gate) shows one offline activity per letter and lets parents mute music, disable tracing, or set a session-end reminder — the only place a "time" concept exists.
24. Voice is a real child-directed Urdu speaker recorded per letter; the same voice for all 38 letters; interruptible on replay.
25. Before any release, run the "healthy hooks vs dark patterns" table as a checklist and record the pass in `review/`.
